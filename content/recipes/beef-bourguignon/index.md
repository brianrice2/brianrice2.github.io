This is pretty much just the Joy of Cooking recipe :)

## Day 1

### Ingredients

- 3 lbs boneless beef stew meat (chuck, brisket, or bottom round), cut into 2" cubes
- 750 mL bottle dry red wine (Pinot noir or Beaujolais)
- 2 Tbsp olive oil
- 1 onion, cut into wedges
- 1 carrot, thickly sliced
- 1 celery rib, thickly sliced
- 3 garlic cloves, smashed
- 4 sprigs parsley
- 3 sprigs thyme, or 1/2 tsp dried
- 1 tsp cracked black peppercorns
- 1 bay leaf
- 1/2 tsp salt

### Preparation

Stir to combine and coat the meat. Cover and marinate in the fridge overnight.

## Day 2

### Ingredients

- 4 oz bacon, diced
- 2 Tbsp all-purpose flour
- 3 Tbsp butter
- 2 cups (8 oz) pearl onions, peeled
- 8 oz mushrooms, sliced
- 1/4 cup chopped parsley

### Preparation

Drain the beef, reserving the marinade, and pat dry. Strain the marinade and reserve the liquid and vegetables separately.

In a large Dutch oven, cook the bacon until brown and rendered. Transfer to a plate, leaving the fat.
Add the meat in batches (3?) and brown on all sides. Transfer to the bacon plate.
Next, add the veggies and cook until lightly browned, around 8 minutes. Stir in the flour until it starts to brown, around a minute. 
Add the marinade, beef, and bacon back to the pot. Add extra wine or water if the beef and veggies aren't covered.

Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat, cover, and simmer gently (or bake at 300ºF) until the meat is fork-tender, about 1.5-2 hours.

In the meantime, prepare the onions and mushrooms: Melt the butter in a pan and add the pearl onions, stirring often, until lightly browned and just tender, about 5-8 minutes. Then add the mushrooms until their juices are cooked off. Add to the stew, along with the chopped parsley and salt/pepper to taste.

When the stew is done, remove the herb sprigs and bay leaf. Chill overnight if you like. Skim off any fat from the surface of the stew, and bring back to a simmer if you chilled it. If it's too thin, thicken with butter/flour, or if it's too winey, simmer uncovered for 10-15 minutes to boil it off some.

Serve with thick, toasted slices of baguette or other crusty bread.
