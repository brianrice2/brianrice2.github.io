## Ingredients

### Dough

- 360g all-purpose flour
- 11g salt
- 3g (1 tsp) instant yeast (if using active dry, use 4g and proof it first)
- 4g (1 tsp) granulated sugar
- 285g warm water
- 18g olive oil
- Cinnamon-sugar mix: 2 Tbsp brown sugar plus 1½ tsp cinnamon, stirred together and split in half

### Pan and topping

- 36g olive oil, divided
- 70g light brown sugar
- 2 Tbsp (28g) softened butter
- 1 Tbsp cinnamon

### Glaze

- ¾ cup (85g) powdered sugar
- 1½ Tbsp milk or cream
- ½ tsp vanilla
- Pinch of salt

## Preparation

1. Whisk the flour, salt, yeast, and granulated sugar in a large bowl. Add the water and oil and stir until no dry spots remain. Cover and rest 15 minutes.
2. Do a "bowl fold" with a wet hand: grab the dough from one edge, lift it, and press it into the center, working around the bowl (8–12 grabs). Flip the dough over, cover, and rest 15 minutes.
3. Repeat the fold. Rest 15 minutes.
4. Sprinkle half the cinnamon-sugar over the dough and do a third fold, tucking it in. Rest 15 minutes.
5. Sprinkle on the rest of the cinnamon-sugar and do a fourth fold. Cover and let rise at room temperature (70–75°F) for 1 hour, until nearly doubled and puffy.
6. Line a 9" square metal pan with parchment, leaving some overhang, and lightly grease it. Add 1 Tbsp olive oil and tilt to coat. Gently scrape the dough into the center and drizzle with another Tbsp of oil. Cover and rise 1–1½ hours, until jiggly and nearly filling the pan.
7. While it rises, mash the brown sugar, butter, and cinnamon into a thick paste. Preheat the oven to 450°F with a rack in the lower third.
8. Oil your fingers and press straight down to the bottom of the pan to make dimples about 1½" apart. Spread the brown sugar paste over the top, filling in the dimples, then dimple again to push it in. Drizzle on the last Tbsp of oil.
9. Bake 15–20 minutes, until the topping is bubbly and any exposed dough is deep golden. Tent with foil after 15 minutes if it's browning too fast.
10. Lift it out with the parchment onto a rack. Stir the glaze until it's about as thick as honey (add a few drops of milk if it's too stiff). Drizzle over the warm focaccia and let it set 10–15 minutes before slicing.

## Notes

- **Pan:** Use metal, not glass, which bakes unevenly and gives pale, soft edges. A 10" cast iron skillet works (slightly taller), as does a 9×13 pan or quarter-sheet pan (thinner, and the dough won't reach the corners).
- **Make-ahead:** After the fourth fold, refrigerate the dough 8–24 hours. Then go straight to step 6, but expect the pan rise to take 2–3 hours.
- **Leftovers:** Best the day it's made. Wrap loosely in foil at room temperature and reheat. Also good as French toast the next day.
- **Powdered sugar:** If you only have granulated, blend it in a blender or spice grinder for about a minute until fine, then measure.
